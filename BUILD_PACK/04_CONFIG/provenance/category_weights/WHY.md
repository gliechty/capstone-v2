# Category Weights — Methodology Rationale

**Source workbook:** `CategoryWeights.xlsx`  
**Source tab:** `why`  
**Status:** Provenance record; source wording preserved verbatim.

## Original rationale

Category weights exist to ensure the overall assessment reflects a balanced view of sustainability performance across major dimensions, rather than being dominated by whichever category happens to have the most indicators or strongest signals in the underlying data. While base weights determine the importance of individual indicators (based on how frequently and consistently they appear across source documents), and multipliers adjust those weights based on context (such as industry, market, buyer, or business goal), category weights operate at a higher level. They normalize the final score across People, Governance, Community, and Resources, ensuring that no single category overwhelms the assessment simply due to data density or reporting bias. This is especially important because sustainability frameworks tend to overrepresent certain areas (e.g., emissions or reporting), which does not necessarily align with a holistic view of SME performance.

In contrast to base weights and multipliers, which are applied at the indicator level, category weights are applied at the aggregation stage. Base weights capture how important a specific concept is within the total dataset; multipliers refine that importance based on contextual relevance; and category weights then rebalance the combined results to reflect industry-specific priorities across categories. For example, a manufacturing SME may legitimately score more heavily on resource-related indicators due to operational intensity, while a services firm may be more governance- or people-driven. Category weights encode this difference explicitly, ensuring that final scores remain comparable, interpretable, and aligned with real-world expectations of what “good performance” looks like in each industry.
