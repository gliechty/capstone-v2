# Multiplier Rules — Methodology Rationale

**Source workbook:** `MultiplierRules.xlsx`  
**Source tab:** `why`  
**Status:** Provenance record; source wording preserved verbatim.

## Original rationale

Multiplier rules exist to ensure that the scoring system reflects context-specific materiality, rather than treating all indicators as equally important across all SMEs. While base weights capture the general importance of an indicator based on how frequently it appears across frameworks and documents, they do not account for the fact that certain issues matter more in specific contexts. Multipliers adjust for this by increasing (or occasionally decreasing) the weight of indicators when there is strong evidence that they are particularly relevant for a given industry, market, buyer type, or business goal. For example, energy and emissions indicators carry greater importance in manufacturing, while traceability and reporting become more critical for SMEs targeting EU markets or enterprise buyers. In this way, multipliers ensure that the system responds dynamically to real-world conditions rather than applying a static, one-size-fits-all model.

Unlike base weights, which are derived from overall frequency, multiplier rules are based on patterns of concentration and contextual relevance within the data (for industry and market), as well as designed logic tied to user intent (for buyer type and business goal). They are applied at the indicator level, modifying base weights before aggregation, and are intentionally bounded within a moderate range so that they influence—but do not override—the underlying importance of each indicator. This allows the model to remain grounded in evidence while still capturing nuance. In practice, multiplier rules act as the system’s “context engine,” ensuring that the final assessment reflects not just what matters in general, but what matters most for this specific SME, in this specific situation.
