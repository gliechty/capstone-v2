Let’s follow one answer.

MOSAIC uses three layers of weighting.

First: how strongly a topic is represented in the source documents.

Second: how relevant that question is to this business’s context.

Third: how much its category contributes to the overall result for this industry.

The system sets all three from its configuration.

Take a fictional food business in Peru.

It’s asked: What percentage of your waste is diverted from landfill?

The answer is fifty percent.

First, the answer becomes credit, from zero to one hundred. Here, fifty percent becomes fifty points.

Other numerical questions use their configured benchmarks; choice questions use predefined answer scales.

Layer one, the base weight, is four point oh eight: set by how widely and how often the topic appears across the source documents.

Layer two, the context multiplier, comes from rules that match the business’s industry, markets, buyers and goals.

In this example, the applicable context multiplier is one point one five.

The current configured rules increase importance where they apply.

Credit times the adjusted weight gives two hundred and thirty-four point six points, out of a possible four hundred and sixty-nine point two.

A question left blank drops out of the possible total, rather than counting as zero.

Within each category, earned points are divided by possible points to produce its percentage.

Adding earned points across all categories, then dividing by their combined possible points, gives the raw overall score.

The first two layers shape both this raw score and the industry-weighted score.

Layer three, the category weight, applies after the category percentages are calculated, and only to the industry-weighted score.

For food and agro-processing, Resources carries approximately forty-nine percent, Governance thirty-one, and People and Community about ten each.

Multiplied and added together, they give the industry-weighted score.

Under the same configuration and business context, the same answers always give the same calculated scores.
