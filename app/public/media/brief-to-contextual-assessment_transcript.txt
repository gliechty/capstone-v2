From a capstone brief to a contextual assessment (about 84 seconds). Illustrative animation.

MOSAIC began as a capstone project in Columbia University’s Master of Science in Sustainability Management program, with a simple-sounding brief:

build a global sustainability rating system for small and medium enterprises.

But what should a global rating measure?

A business can face international frameworks, buyer questionnaires and local expectations, all at once.

They don't use the same words, ask for the same evidence, or weigh the same issues equally.

One universal checklist would miss what makes each business different.

So the team built a different kind of system.

First, standards are broken into individual requirements, each traced back to its source.

Next, the business's context decides which questions apply, and which matter more.

Then fixed rules calculate the scores, the same way for every business.

AI has two bounded jobs: helping prepare the sources, and explaining the results.

Because the method is separate from its sources, it could be configured for any set of standards.

A large buyer or an industry association could generate its own assessment: questions, weights and scoring, built from the standards it cares about most.
